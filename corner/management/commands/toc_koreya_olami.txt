# Koreya olami — the shelf pupils open because they WANT to
#
# The sister shelf of "Prime Korean Readings", and its opposite in the same way
# "Matematika olami" is the opposite of "Prime Math Readings": these texts belong to
# NO lesson. Nothing here has to teach PK-24's pattern, nothing has to stay inside what
# has been taught. They exist so that a pupil who came to drill 받침 stays for half an
# hour reading about why the alphabet is shaped like a mouth.
#
#   * NO `order` link to a lesson (order is just this shelf's own reading order).
#   * NO `"stories"` key pointing here from any tutorial.
#   * NO cumulative restriction — mention any pattern, as long as the text explains it.
#   * NO audio. This shelf's prose is UZBEK, so a Korean voice cannot read it and the
#     Korean words in it are single words, not sentences. Never run gen_corner_audio
#     here. (Prime Korean Readings keep theirs — those are Korean texts.)
#
# ── ⚠️ THE LANGUAGE SPLIT — the one thing that makes this shelf different ──
#
# Every other Korean collection is a Korean TEXT with Uzbek glosses. This one inverts
# it: the prose is **Uzbek**, and Korean appears as the MATERIAL being talked about —
# single words and short phrases, each one a `cn-word` span with an Uzbek `data-tr`.
#
#   <span class="cn-word" data-tr="chiqish joyi">출구</span>
#
# A reader who cannot yet read Hangul must still be able to finish the text. So:
#   * every Korean word is glossed the first time, in the span itself;
#   * give the Uzbek pronunciation in brackets the first time a word matters —
#     출구 (chulgu) — spelled the way `storyvideo/korean.py` spells it, so the shelf,
#     the video and the narration never disagree with each other;
#   * 한자 may be shown beside a Sino-Korean word (出口) but is never glossed alone
#     and never appears in a sentence the reader is meant to read aloud.
#
# ── The four families, and why each one is here ──
#
#   TIL QANDAY ISHLAYDI — the strongest card this shelf holds, because it is the one
#     an Uzbek pupil cannot get from an English-language Korean channel. Sino-Korean
#     root families (출/出 → 출구·출근·출발). Word order, which Uzbek and Korean SHARE
#     and English does not. Kelishik ↔ 조사. Two number systems. Speech levels.
#   ALIFBO VA TOVUSH — Hangul as a designed object: 1443, Sejong, letters shaped like
#     the organ that makes them, 받침, why syllables are written in blocks.
#   KOREYA VA TARIX — real people and real dates: Sejong, 훈민정음 해례본 and UNESCO,
#     Hanja's arrival and retreat, how Korean was written before 1443.
#   HAYOT VA MADANIYAT — 존댓말 at a shop counter, why 4 is avoided, Konglish, names
#     and family words, what a Korean classroom looks like. Facts must be true.
#
# ── Shape of a text ──
#
#   200–320 words. A hook in the first two sentences (a question, a scene, a claim the
#   reader wants to argue with), then the idea, then the pay-off. One idea per text.
#   Chronological, one thing per sentence, exactly as in the readings toc.
#
#   cn-word  →  8–14 KOREAN words, data-tr = a short Uzbek meaning.
#   grammar  →  the IDEA behind the text, named: the root family, the word order rule,
#               the letter-shape principle. Not a TOPIK pattern unless the text is
#               actually about one. `examples` may be Korean or Uzbek↔Korean pairs.
#   questions → 2–3 in Uzbek: at least one comprehension, and at least one that makes
#               the reader USE the idea on a word the text did not gloss. Story
#               questions render in STORED ORDER, so vary `answer` by hand.
#   summary  →  one Uzbek sentence. If the text retells real material, say so there.
#   open_question → one TRANSFER question with no answer anywhere in the text. It is
#               also the video's last card. See the storyvideo notes below.
#
# ── ⚠️ FACTS ──
#   Every date, name and number is checked before it ships. 1443 is when the alphabet
#   was finished; 1446 is when 훈민정음 was published — do not merge them. Where a
#   figure is an estimate ("yarmidan koʻpi"), say so rather than inventing a percent.
#   Where a claim about a letter's shape comes from 훈민정음 해례본, that is a real
#   source and may be stated as fact; folk etymologies may not.
#
# ── This shelf is a video source (storyvideo/) ──
#   These texts feed the `ko*` films in `storyvideo/stories/`. What films well here is
#   not a story arc but a STRUCTURE the picture can count: a root family (nine words
#   from one syllable), a three-way word-order comparison, a syllable being built.
#   So prefer a text whose idea has a NUMBER in it, and put that number in the first
#   two sentences. The rules in toc_prime_math_readings.txt about the `ask` gap and
#   the quotable closing line apply here unchanged.
#
# Rhythm: written in small batches (2–3 texts), or on request ("make 3 more Koreya
# olami stories"). Mark finished ones [done].

## Koreya olami

### Til qanday ishlaydi (1–12)
1.  Bitta boʻgʻin — toʻqqizta soʻz (출/出 ildiz oilasi)                        [done]
2.  Oʻzbek va koreys: bir xil tartib                                          [done]
3.  Koreys tilida nega ikkita sanoq tizimi bor
4.  Kelishik va 조사: -ni, -ga, -ning koreyschada
5.  Bir soʻzning uch xil darajasi: 반말, 존댓말, 높임말
6.  «Men» soʻzi koreyschada nega ikkita (저 va 나)
7.  Fe'l oxirida turadi — va shuning uchun oxirigacha eshitish kerak
8.  Koreys tilida koʻplik qoʻshimchasi nega majburiy emas
9.  Rang nomlari: 파랗다 ham koʻk, ham yashil
10. 한자어 va 고유어: bir maʼno, ikki soʻz
11. Konglish: inglizchadan kelib, koreyschaga aylangan soʻzlar
12. Ismlar: familiya avval, va nega Kim juda koʻp

### Alifbo va tovush (13–20)

16. Yettita tovush: 받침 qanday soddalashadi
17. ㅗ va ㅓ — oʻzbek tilidagi oʻ va o
18. Nega 감사합니다 «kamsahapnida» emas, «kamsahamnida»
19. Yozuvsiz xalq: 1443-yilgacha koreyslar qanday yozgan
20. Alifbo bayrami — 10-oktabr

### Koreya va tarix (21–28)
